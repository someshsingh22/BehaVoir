\section{Introduction}
Progress in computational social science is gated by clean observational datasets: environments such as
r/ChangeMyView deltas or r/AmItheAsshole verdicts took researchers months to years to find, filter and label.
Because curation is expensive, it gravitates to the head of the distribution, and the long tail of
phenomenon~$\times$~audience~$\times$~context combinations stays unstudied. LLM agents can amortize this cost into
compute, turning a research question into a curated dataset on demand. Goal-driven dataset discovery is therefore a
prerequisite for scaling both human and automated science~\cite{gupta_accelerating_2026}; our goal is to measure
whether agents can already do it.

Dataset quality is hard to judge in isolation, so we ground evaluation in published papers that released their code
(Table~\ref{tab:environments}). The agent sees only the research question, a fixed raw corpus snapshot with its
documentation, and a compute budget; the resulting dataset is scored by downstream utility, both with the authors'
own pipeline and with an open, standardized pipeline against the authors' dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:approach}). This
makes the question falsifiable---\emph{can an agent, under a time budget, match datasets experts spent months
building?}---and exposes characteristic failure modes such as hallucinated schemas, prompt anchoring and label
leakage, giving a yardstick to track progress.

\paragraph{Results so far.} On Reddit, our agent-built data beats Winning Args on Dr.~Inventor argument mining
(+1.82 F1 on average) and improves MASIVE affect identification by 20\%. Both affect runs are still thread-centric:
11.36k threads / 171.7k comments convert to only 15.17k train + 801 val MASIVE examples (4.41k unique affect
targets), and EmoEvent performance concentrates on \emph{happy}/\emph{sad}, suggesting many collected replies are
neutral advice; we are moving to affect-centric collection. On AlignX, AutoData trails the original (62.75\% vs.\
65.02\% pooled; +2.82 on P-SOUPS concise/detail, $-$7.22 on PRISM). The gap persists with 5K unique examples each
(62.52\% vs.\ 67.81\%), ruling out duplication from reversed pairs; the most likely cause is persona richness
(median 34 vs.\ 55 words), and we are retraining on LLM-enriched personas. On GitHub, our Decompile-Bench v2 data
beats DCBench-1.3B on HumanEval re-executability (22.56 vs.\ 20.66) and on HumanEval/MBPP edit similarity, winning at
O1--O3 but not O0 (Table~\ref{tab:decompile}); it trails on GitHub2025 after decontamination removed much of our
training data. For RepairLLaMA our baseline does not yet match the paper's model and a larger run is in progress.
